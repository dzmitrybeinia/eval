# Lesson name: First Aid Basics: Understanding Common Injuries and How to Respond

## Intro

### Introduction to First Aid Basics

First aid is the immediate care provided to someone who is injured or experiencing a medical emergency before professional help arrives. Knowing basic first aid can make a huge difference in saving lives and preventing further harm. In this lesson, we will explore the steps to respond to three common injuries: cuts, burns, and choking. Let's get ready to learn how to act confidently in emergencies! 🩹🔥🍽

### Responding to Cuts

A cut is an injury where the skin is broken, often caused by sharp objects. Cuts can vary from minor scrapes to deeper wounds. To respond effectively:

1. Stay calm to manage the situation effectively.
2. Wash your hands to prevent infection.
3. Stop the bleeding by applying gentle pressure with a clean cloth or bandage.
4. Clean the wound using clean water to remove dirt or debris.
5. Cover the wound with a sterile bandage.
6. Seek medical help for deep cuts or if bleeding persists.

#### **Themes:**
- Steps to treat cuts

## YesNo

**Statement:** To treat a cut, you should immediately start by washing the wound with soap and water before applying pressure to stop the bleeding.

**Answer:** False

**Feedback:**
The correct sequence begins with applying gentle pressure to stop the bleeding, followed by cleaning the wound with clean water. Washing with soap should not be the initial step as it may introduce bacteria to the wound.

### Responding to Burns

Burns occur when the skin is damaged by heat, chemicals, or electricity. They are categorized as first-degree (minor), second-degree, and third-degree (severe). To respond:

1. Stay calm to assist effectively.
2. Cool the burn under cool water for 10-15 minutes.
3. Protect the burn with a clean, non-stick bandage or cloth.
4. Avoid applying butter, oils, or ointments, and do not break blisters.
5. Seek medical help for large burns or those on sensitive areas like the face or hands.

#### **Themes:**
- Steps to treat burns

## YesNo

**Statement:** Cooling the burn area under cool water is an immediate step to take after the injury.

**Answer:** True

**Feedback:**
The initial action of running cool water over a burn helps to lessen the severity of the injury by reducing the temperature of the skin and surrounding areas, which can prevent further damage and reduce pain.

### Responding to Choking

Choking occurs when an object blocks the airway, making breathing difficult. Act quickly:

1. Recognize signs like clutching the throat or difficulty speaking.
2. Encourage coughing if the person can still breathe or talk.
3. Perform the Heimlich maneuver if the person cannot cough out the object.
4. Call emergency services if the object remains stuck or the person becomes unconscious.

#### **Themes:**
- Steps to treat choking

## YesNo

**Statement:** If someone is choking, you should immediately perform the Heimlich maneuver to dislodge the object blocking their airway.

**Answer:** True

**Feedback:**
In a situation where an individual is unable to cough or breathe due to an obstruction, the Heimlich maneuver is a recommended first aid technique to clear the airway obstruction. It involves standing behind the person, placing your hands just below the rib cage, and delivering a quick upward abdominal thrust.


## Content slide

### Summary: First Aid Basics

Cuts, burns, and choking are common injuries requiring immediate attention. Key points:

- For cuts, focus on stopping the bleeding, cleaning, and covering the wound.
- For burns, cool the area, protect it, and avoid harmful remedies.
- For choking, encourage coughing or perform the Heimlich maneuver.

Staying calm and knowing when to seek professional help are essential in all first aid situations.

#### **Themes:**
- Summary of first aid responses

## YesNo

**Statement:** In the event of a burn, it is advisable to immediately apply ice to the affected area.

**Answer:** False

**Feedback:**
The recommended approach for treating burns is to cool the area with cool water, not ice, to prevent further damage to the skin and underlying tissues.
